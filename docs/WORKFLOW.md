# Scientific workflow

The study follows a one-way evidence and design-decision chain.

1. **Source-qualified properties and evidence.** Fluid-property correlations are evaluated only within their reported validity intervals. Skeleton and nanoparticle records retain source, composition, loading, temperature, and claim-boundary metadata.
2. **Unit-scale transient response.** The local-thermal-nonequilibrium model solves charge and counterflow discharge for one packed-bed module. Qualified energy is the time integral of discharge energy while the normalized outlet-temperature grade remains above the registered threshold.
3. **Modular service closure.** For each registered prescribed power–energy requirement, the total mass flow is resolved and divided among an integer number of parallel modules. The installed energy is then re-evaluated at that per-module flow. The reported module count is the smallest feasible integer.
4. **Design-consequence boundary.** The V18 layer introduces continuous module requirements and an independent integer-reclosure audit. It distinguishes a preserved ordering in continuous performance from a difference large enough to change an integer configuration.
5. **Robustness and communication.** Resolution thresholds, numerical refinement, sampling, model-form stress, property uncertainty, standby loss, and external validation bound the interpretation. The postprocessing workflow then assembles the results into six main and nine supplementary figures.

The scientific conclusion is intentionally limited: a material contrast can preserve its continuous ordering while no longer changing the modular-system decision after a nominal-power lower bound and integer closure are imposed. The code does not translate source-specific skeleton or nanoparticle evidence into an unsupported universal material ranking.
